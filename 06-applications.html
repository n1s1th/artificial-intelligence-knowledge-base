Applications of Artificial Intelligence

Where AI is actually used — from healthcare and gaming to finance, military, software development, and web search. A survey of AI in practice, with examples, capabilities, and limitations.

AI is everywhere — but not equally effective.

AI and machine learning technology is used in most of the essential applications of the 2020s. However, the degree of capability varies enormously across domains. Some applications are mature and reliable; others are experimental or unreliable. Understanding where AI works well — and where it doesn't — is essential for evaluating real-world AI systems.

01 — Overview

AI applications across domains.

Figure 06 · AI is deployed across many domains, with varying degrees of maturity and reliability
Not all applications are equal

Some AI applications are mature and reliable (spam filters, recommendation systems), while others are experimental or unreliable (autonomous vehicles, medical diagnosis). The maturity of an application depends on the availability of training data, the clarity of the task, and the cost of errors.

02 — Software Development

AI-assisted coding.

AI tools like GitHub Copilot, Cursor, and Codex have transformed software development by generating code from natural-language descriptions, completing partial code, and explaining existing code.

Current capabilities

AI code assistants can generate boilerplate code, translate between languages, write tests, debug errors, and explain complex code. They are particularly effective for repetitive tasks, standard patterns, and well-documented APIs.

Adoption: Surveys suggest 70-90% of professional developers now use AI coding assistants regularly.

Limitations

AI-generated code can contain subtle bugs, security vulnerabilities, or performance issues. Complex logic, novel algorithms, and domain-specific optimizations still require human expertise. Code assistants can also hallucinate non-existent libraries or APIs.

Best practice: Treat AI-generated code as a starting point, not a final product. Always review, test, and understand the code before deploying it.

The productivity paradox

While AI coding assistants increase individual productivity, they may also increase the volume of code that needs review, testing, and maintenance. The net effect on software quality and development velocity is still being studied.

03 — Chatbots

Conversational AI.

Chatbots and virtual assistants have become one of the most visible applications of AI. From customer service to personal assistants, conversational AI is deployed across industries.

Customer service

AI chatbots handle routine inquiries, route complex cases to humans, and provide 24/7 availability. They reduce costs but can frustrate users when they fail to understand nuanced questions.

Virtual assistants

Siri, Alexa, Google Assistant — AI systems that respond to voice commands, control smart devices, and provide information. They excel at simple tasks but struggle with complex, multi-step requests.

Therapeutic chatbots

AI systems designed to provide emotional support or cognitive behavioral therapy. Controversial — they can provide accessible support but lack genuine empathy and clinical judgment.

The ELIZA effect

Humans tend to attribute understanding and emotion to systems that produce human-like responses, even when those systems have no comprehension. This can lead to over-trust and emotional dependence on AI systems that do not actually understand users. → Note 07: Ethics (substitution for human interaction)

04 — Healthcare

AI in health and medicine.

AlphaFold 2 (2021) demonstrated the ability to approximate, in hours rather than months, the 3D structure of a protein. In 2023, it was reported that AI-assisted diagnosis matched or exceeded human performance in some medical imaging tasks.

AlphaFold
A deep learning system by Google DeepMind that predicts protein 3D structures from amino acid sequences with near-experimental accuracy. It solved a 50-year grand challenge in biology and has accelerated drug discovery and disease research.

High stakes, high potential

Healthcare AI has enormous potential to improve outcomes and reduce costs, but errors can be life-threatening. Regulatory approval, clinical validation, and human oversight are essential. AI should augment, not replace, medical professionals.

05 — Gaming

AI in games: from chess to Go to StarCraft.

Game playing programs have been used since the 1950s to demonstrate and test AI's most advanced techniques. Games provide controlled environments with clear objectives, making them ideal testbeds for AI research.

Figure 07 · Major AI milestones in gaming — from chess to real-time strategy games

Deep Blue (1997)

IBM's chess computer defeated world champion Garry Kasparov in a six-game match. It used brute-force search with hand-crafted evaluation functions, examining 200 million positions per second. This demonstrated that AI could master complex strategy games.

AlphaGo (2016)

Google DeepMind's program defeated world Go champion Lee Sedol 4-1. Unlike Deep Blue, AlphaGo used deep learning and reinforcement learning, training on millions of games and learning intuitive pattern recognition. Go was considered far harder than chess due to its larger state space.

Why games matter

Games are microcosms of real-world problems — they require planning, perception, adaptation, and competition. AI breakthroughs in games often translate to practical applications. The techniques developed for AlphaGo are now used in protein folding, materials science, and robotics.

06 — Mathematics

AI in mathematical research.

In mathematics, probabilistic large language models are versatile but can also produce wrong answers in the form of hallucinations. The Alibaba Group developed a version of its Qwen model that can solve mathematical problems and prove theorems.

AlphaDev

DeepMind's system discovered faster sorting algorithms by treating algorithm design as a game. It found sorting routines that are 70% faster than those in standard libraries for small arrays. This demonstrates AI's ability to improve fundamental algorithms.

Theorem proving

AI systems can verify mathematical proofs, suggest proof strategies, and even discover new theorems. However, they can also produce plausible-sounding but incorrect proofs. Human verification remains essential for important results.

Hallucination in mathematics

LLMs can produce confident but incorrect mathematical reasoning. They may state false theorems, make subtle logical errors, or invent non-existent mathematical objects. For mathematics, where correctness is paramount, AI assistance requires careful human verification.

07 — Finance

AI in financial markets.

AI systems are widely used in finance for algorithmic trading, fraud detection, credit scoring, and risk assessment. High-frequency trading algorithms execute millions of trades per second based on market signals.

Algorithmic trading

AI systems analyze market data, news sentiment, and technical indicators to make trading decisions faster than humans. They account for a significant portion of trading volume in major markets.

Risk: Algorithmic trading can amplify market volatility and contribute to flash crashes when multiple systems react to the same signals.

Fraud detection

Machine learning models detect anomalous transactions that may indicate fraud, money laundering, or identity theft. They process millions of transactions per day, flagging suspicious activity for human review.

Challenge: Balancing false positives (legitimate transactions flagged as fraud) against false negatives (fraud that goes undetected).

08 — Military

AI in defense and warfare.

AI is increasingly used in military applications including surveillance, logistics, autonomous vehicles, and weapons systems. These applications raise significant ethical concerns.

Current applications

AI is used for intelligence analysis, logistics optimization, predictive maintenance, and autonomous drones. Military forces are investing heavily in AI capabilities, viewing them as strategically important.

Lethal autonomous weapons

Systems that can select and engage targets without human oversight. Several countries are developing such systems, raising concerns about accountability, escalation, and the dehumanization of warfare.

09 — Generative AI

Creating content with AI.

Generative AI systems can produce text, images, audio, video, and code. These systems have transformed content creation, design, and creative workflows across industries.

Text generation

LLMs write articles, emails, code, marketing copy, and creative fiction. Used in content marketing, customer service, education, and personal productivity.

Image generation

Diffusion models create images from text descriptions. Used in advertising, design, entertainment, and art. Raises questions about copyright and authenticity.

Audio & video

AI generates speech, music, and video content. Used in entertainment, education, and accessibility. Deepfakes pose significant misinformation risks.

The creativity question

Can AI be truly creative, or is it merely recombining patterns from training data? Generative AI produces novel outputs, but whether this constitutes creativity or sophisticated mimicry remains debated. → Note 09: Philosophy

10 — Agents

AI agents in practice.

AI agents are software entities designed to perceive their environment, make decisions, and take actions autonomously to achieve specific goals. They represent a shift from single-turn chat to multi-step, goal-oriented systems.

Current applications

Agents are being deployed for research (searching and synthesizing information), software development (writing and testing code), customer service (handling complex multi-step inquiries), and personal assistance (managing calendars, email, and tasks).

Multi-agent systems

Frameworks like CrewAI enable multiple agents to collaborate on complex tasks, dividing work and coordinating actions. This is an emerging area with significant potential but also challenges in coordination and reliability.

The agentic future

Many researchers believe agents represent the next major evolution in AI — moving from models that answer questions to systems that take action. The transition is underway but faces challenges in reliability, safety, and human oversight. → Note 05: Modern AI Systems (agents)

11 — Web Search

AI in search engines.

AI Overviews and similar features use large language models to provide synthesized answers to search queries, rather than just lists of links. This represents a fundamental shift in how people find information online.

AI-powered search

Modern search engines use AI to understand query intent, rank results, and generate direct answers. Google's AI Overviews, Bing's Copilot, and Perplexity provide conversational search experiences.

Challenges

AI-generated search answers can contain errors, hallucinations, or outdated information. They may also reduce traffic to source websites, raising concerns about the sustainability of web content creation.

12 — Other Industries

AI across industries.

In a 2017 survey, one in five companies reported having incorporated "AI" in some offerings or processes. AI is now used in virtually every industry, though the depth and sophistication of deployment varies widely.

Autonomous vehicles

Self-driving cars use computer vision, sensor fusion, and planning algorithms. Still experimental; full autonomy remains a challenge due to edge cases and safety requirements.

Agriculture

AI optimizes crop yields, detects diseases, automates harvesting, and predicts weather impacts. Precision agriculture reduces waste and improves sustainability.

Manufacturing

Predictive maintenance, quality control, supply chain optimization, and robotic automation. AI improves efficiency and reduces downtime.

Education

Personalized learning, automated grading, intelligent tutoring systems. Potential to improve access but raises concerns about data privacy and the role of human teachers.

Legal

Contract analysis, legal research, document review. AI assists lawyers but cannot replace legal judgment and advocacy.

Energy

Grid optimization, predictive maintenance, renewable energy forecasting. AI helps integrate variable renewable sources into power grids.
